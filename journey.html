MY JOURNEY

A story of receiving, learning, leading and creating.

This is not a story about having everything figured out. It is a story about what happened when opportunities met curiosity.

BEFORE THE TITLES

There was just a girl who wanted to learn.

I grew up in Makoko, Lagos, in a family of nine. My father is a tailor and my mother sells fish.

Growing up in a riverine community exposed me to both the challenges and the strength that exist in underserved communities.

01

EDUCATION

The opportunity that changed everything.

Before I started school, I watched my older sisters go.

They had uniforms and backpacks. They came home with assignments. They made friends. I wanted to be able to write and recite ABCD and 123 just like them.

My father eventually enrolled me, and from Primary 1, Slum2School became a major part of my educational journey.

I attended Makoko Anglican Primary School and continued receiving educational support through Slum2School for almost a decade.

02

SLUM2SCHOOL

I didn't just receive an education. I found people.

Slum2School became much more than school support.

Mentors, staff, volunteers and fellow scholars became part of my development.

People like Mr Henry, Mr Adekunle and Mr Orondaam, among others, guided me at different points.

I experienced mentorship, STEM programmes, summer programmes, leadership opportunities and exposure to spaces outside my community.

Today, I still support younger learners where I can — including mentoring learners and helping staff with learner stories and information.

03

TECHNOLOGY

The girl who wanted to build discovered technology.

Then STEM programmes introduced me to coding and technology.

I began exploring programming, web development and other digital skills. I learned through STEM programmes, self-directed learning and digital training, building experience with technologies such as HTML, CSS, JavaScript, SQL and Microsoft Power BI.

Eventually, my interests began moving toward data and the ways technology can be used to solve real-world problems.

04

LEADERSHIP

I began discovering that my voice could take up space.

In secondary school, I served as President of the Leadership Club, Time Keeper and Computer Laboratory Prefect.

I also participated in youth leadership experiences such as the Pan African Youth Leadership Program.

PAYLP took me to Atlanta, Burlington and Washington, D.C., where I experienced leadership development, cultural exchange and conversations with young people from different backgrounds.

These experiences helped me think more intentionally about leadership and the kind of person I want to become.

05

GIRLS' EMPOWERMENT

Eventually, I stopped asking “What can I do?” and started asking “Who can I help?”

My experiences as a girl shaped the way I think about girls' empowerment.

Through Girls in Bloom, I became involved in conversations around girls, leadership, health and technology.

I co-moderated a Girls X Technology conversation at the 2024 Girls in Bloom Global Summit and later became a Global Ambassador.

I also helped lead Bloom With Dignity, a project focused on menstrual health, hygiene, access to menstrual products and HPV vaccine awareness.

06

USING MY VOICE

I started speaking not because I knew everything, but because I had something to share.

I have spoken in school outreaches, panels, youth conversations, mentorship sessions and leadership spaces.

One of the experiences that stretched me was participating as a panelist at the Slum2School Global Volunteer Summit in 2023.

Public speaking continues to be something I am growing into.

07

LEARNING TO TEACH

From learner to facilitator.

Through Hello Future, I received training to facilitate digital skills sessions.

The experience helped me understand that knowing something and being able to teach it clearly are two different skills.

I learned to communicate digital concepts through Digital Foundations and Storytelling for Marketing and Advocacy.

08

CREATING SPACE

Girls Unplugged started from my bed.

I wanted girls to have a space where they could meet other girls, talk openly, learn and grow together.

So I created Girls Unplugged — Bloom & Glow Circle.

It became a reminder that you do not always need perfect conditions to start something.

Sometimes you start with what you have.

09

WHAT COMES NEXT

I am still becoming.

I am interested in the intersection of technology, data, education, leadership and social impact.

I want to become a globally recognised public speaker and a renowned technologist, particularly in data.

I also want to keep building things that make opportunities easier to find and more accessible to young people.

LUMINDA is one expression of that ambition.

AND THIS IS WHERE I AM NOW

Still learning.
Still building.
Still becoming.

My story is not finished.

Motunrayo Linda Idowu

Youth Leader · Technologist · Speaker · Girls' Empowerment Advocate
